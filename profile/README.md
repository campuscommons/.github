# Campus Commons

Campus Commons is a web platform that a school installs and runs on its own
infrastructure.

Students already spend their time online. Just nowhere connected to school, on
apps built for the entire internet and never with a high school in mind. Campus
Commons is the familiar shape of those apps rebuilt to belong to one campus.
Think Reddit, Instagram, Discord and Wattpad, except everyone posting is someone
you pass in the hallway, and the school can see and moderate what happens.

Schools publish a lot: club pages, a newspaper, student work, notices, the
things people actually go to the site for. Usually that ends up spread across
services the school rents and does not control. This is the version the school
holds the keys to. The accounts are the school's accounts, the domain is the
school's domain, and the database sits in the school's account.

One sign-in covers all of it: the pages students and clubs publish, and the
consoles staff use to run and moderate them. Turn on the parts you want. The
rest stay off until you need them.

It started as one high school's website, built by a student there for the people
he sat next to. It is packaged now so that any school can run its own copy.

## What that means in practice

Everyone inside has a school account, so nobody is posting to a room full of
strangers. Moderation, reporting, audit logs and account security are part of
the platform rather than something a school has to source separately, and most
schools cannot source them at all.

Nobody sits between the school and its site, so there is no vendor account that
can be suspended and no copy of the data held somewhere else.

The documentation is written for whoever ends up maintaining it, which at a
school is usually a teacher or a student rather than a full-time engineer.

Nobody charges the school for the software, and nobody makes money from what
happens on it. That is a condition of the license, not a promise.

## Getting involved

Bug reports, ideas and documentation fixes count as much as code. The
contributing guide in the platform repository covers how to start. Security
problems go through the private reporting process in the security policy, not a
public issue.
